# Oryzhaevan

> The king wore a crown of rain. Every drop remembered a different head.

## The Throne at the Bottom of the Well

The well of Oryzhaevan stands in a courtyard paved with white scales. Nobody knows the creature that shed them. They do not overlap, yet the rain passes between them without reaching the earth. At night, the courtyard can be heard breathing from the rooms above.

A throne rests beneath the water. Its occupant appears only in reflections: a tall figure wearing a crown that falls continuously around its face. The crown never touches its shoulders. Each falling drop becomes a small ring before vanishing into the darkness.

The people above the well call the figure their king. Their histories contain no coronation. Their laws are written in the handwriting of someone who has never learned to hold a pen.

## The Embassy of Pale Wings

Three envoys arrived carrying a length of white silk. Their faces were hidden beneath narrow masks, and their shadows had wings that folded in opposite directions. They asked permission to lower the silk into the well.

The courtyard attendants agreed. The cloth descended for an entire afternoon without reaching the surface. When it was drawn back, its end was embroidered with a small crown and a name none of the envoys recognized.

One mask cracked. Behind it was an eye with a vertical pupil, surrounded by the reflection of a dry courtyard. The envoy covered the crack and asked whether rain had ever fallen there.

The attendants answered yes. The other two envoys left immediately. The third remained beside the well until the silk began to drip upward.

## A Census of Unoccupied Rooms

The palace census lists ninety rooms and ninety-one occupants. Every room is empty when inspected. The occupants appear only in the accounting, where their ages increase whenever a window is closed.

The extra occupant has no age. Its name consists of a circular mark that cannot be copied without leaving a wet stain beneath the paper. The census keeper places a bowl under the desk to collect these stains. The bowl remains dry, but its reflection slowly fills with water.

Once, the keeper erased the circular mark. The courtyard stopped breathing. All ninety windows opened inward, although their hinges had been fitted on the other side.

He restored the mark before sunset. In the well, the king turned toward a room that was absent from the palace plans.

## The Visitor in the Unfinished Room

The plans leave a narrow blank space between two staircases. Builders have attempted to measure it, but every measurement describes the width of the white silk rather than the distance between the walls.

On certain nights, a visitor can be heard walking through that space. Its footsteps are heavy, separated by long pauses. During those pauses, the palace servants hear the faint rubbing of scales against stone.

A servant left a lamp on the nearest stair. The lamp burned without oil until morning. Its light revealed a doorway in the blank space, beyond which hung the same narrow mask the envoy had worn.

The crack had widened. Through it, the servant saw a throne resting in a dry well. Upon that throne sat an empty crown, shedding rain onto a white silk cushion.

## The Inscription That Refuses a Surface

**Ory — zhae — van — ulreth.**

The sounds are preserved in a chant sung while the courtyard is swept. The attendants do not know what they mean. They know that each sound must be followed by a pause long enough for one drop to leave the crown.

A scholar tried to write the chant upon a tablet. The letters appeared on the stone beneath the tablet instead. He placed another stone there, and the writing moved deeper into the courtyard.

Eventually, the letters could be seen only from the rooms above, arranged across the scales like footsteps around the well. The scholar left without retrieving his tablet. In the census, a new occupant appeared with an age measured in pauses.

## The Questions of the Remaining Envoy

> Who sits beneath the falling rings?
>
> The one whose head the rain has not chosen.
>
> Who walks between the staircases?
>
> The room that remembers having a door.
>
> Why does the silk refuse the water?
>
> Because someone below is still holding its other end.

These questions were heard after the envoy removed its mask. No attendant saw the uncovered face. Everyone was watching the shadow, whose wings had spread across the well without darkening its reflection.

The silk disappeared before dawn. The mask remained upon the stair, intact, with no sign of a crack.

## When the Courtyard Holds Its Breath

The king has not left the throne. The census keeper continues placing a dry bowl beneath his desk. The servants avoid closing the final window, though nobody agrees which window that is.

Sometimes a ring of rain rises out of the well and hangs above the scales. Within it, a distant courtyard can be seen beneath a clear sky. A visitor waits there, holding white silk between claws that seem too gentle for their size.

Oryzhaevan remains in the pause before the next drop falls. Beneath the water, the crown continues searching for the head it remembers.

*The rain recognizes a throne the palace cannot contain.*
